The hard truth

We have to be honest — because pretending all is well is its own kind of danger.

The Nigeria many of us grew up in has changed, and the numbers are sobering:

  • The National Bureau of Statistics recorded around 2.2 million kidnapping incidents in a single year (May 2023–April 2024), with families paying roughly ₦2.23 trillion in ransom.
  • Independent trackers logged at least 4,722 people abducted in the year to June 2025 — no part of the country truly untouched.
  • Into 2026, civic groups warned that over 1,000 Nigerians were taken in the first weeks of the year alone.
  • School raids returned with a vengeance — hundreds of children seized in single attacks, spreading well beyond the old northern flashpoints.

These aren't foreign headlines. They are our neighbours, our colleagues, the people we sit beside in church and at the mosque.

But here's the hope

The picture is not hopeless — and you are not helpless.

In that same period, Nigerian banks reported that digital-payment fraud fell by about 51% — from ₦52.26 billion to ₦25.85 billion in a single year. That didn't happen by luck. It happened because people got smarter, more careful, and harder to fool.

That is the entire premise of this playbook. Because every criminal — the armed robber, the "one chance" gang, the kidnap crew, the online fraudster — needs the same three things to win:

Surprise. Isolation. Control.

Take away even one, and most attacks fall apart. Awareness destroys surprise. Good habits deny isolation. A practised plan strips away control. You don't need to be a fighter, wealthy, or armed — you only need to be a harder, less predictable target than the situation around you. And that, every family can become.
